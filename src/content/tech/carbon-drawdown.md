---

Carbon drawdown means pulling carbon dioxide back out of the air on an industrial scale and locking it away for good, by capturing it with machines, by burning plants for energy and burying the CO2, or by spreading crushed rock that absorbs it. At a billion tonnes a year, engineered removal would cancel out a few percent of today's emissions and begin to repair the damage from past ones, which almost every pathway to the Paris goals assumes.

Today the industry is tiny but real. Climeworks' plants in Iceland turn captured CO2 into stone underground. Its Mammoth plant, opened in 2024, is designed for 36,000 tonnes a year but removed only 675 tonnes in the first half of 2026. All such novel methods together remove about 2 million tonnes a year, a five-hundredth of the target, though that total is growing by around 40% a year. The next test is scale: the first quarter-million-tonne plant, Stratos in Texas, was delayed by a repair after commissioning in 2026. Cost, clean energy supply and reliable buyers will decide how fast the rest follows.
