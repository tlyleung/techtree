Longevity escape velocity is the point at which medicine adds remaining life expectancy faster than time passes: for each year you live, treatments buy you more than a year back. The idea, popularised by Aubrey de Grey, is that people do not need a single cure for ageing, only steady progress that outruns it. A country reaching it would mark the moment ageing stopped being the fixed boundary of a human life.

Today life expectancy in the longest-lived countries rises by only a fraction of a year per year, and those gains have slowed since 1990. The case for optimism rests on drugs and gene therapies that target ageing itself. Rapamycin lengthens the lives of mice even when started late, and partial reprogramming, which resets cells toward a younger state, has rejuvenated tissues in mice. In 2026 a reprogramming gene therapy entered its first human trial, for eye disease. No treatment has yet been shown to extend human lifespan, and escape velocity, if it comes, looks most likely in the second half of the century.
