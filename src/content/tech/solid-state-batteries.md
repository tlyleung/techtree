A solid-state battery replaces the flammable liquid electrolyte inside a lithium-ion cell with a solid one, usually a ceramic or sulfide glass. That could allow a lithium-metal anode, giving more range in the same space, faster charging and a much lower fire risk. For electric cars, it is the most anticipated battery upgrade in decades.

The chemistry has been promising since 2011, when Japanese researchers found a solid that conducts lithium about as well as a liquid. Making it work at scale has been harder: the solid layers crack and lose contact as the cell breathes, and they are hard to manufacture cheaply. Since 2025, BMW and Mercedes have been driving test cars with solid-state cells on public roads, and Chinese makers such as BYD say a first model will arrive in 2027. Several Chinese cars already use "semi-solid-state" batteries, which still contain some liquid; this page counts only fully solid cells, and none is yet in a car you can buy.
