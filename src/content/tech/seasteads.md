A seastead is a permanent home on the ocean: floating platforms, linked into a town, that people live on all year round. Advocates see seasteads as a way to make new land without taking it from anyone, to adapt to rising seas, and to try new ways of governing communities. Floating structures are not the hard part. Japan built and flew aircraft from a kilometre-long floating runway in Tokyo Bay around 2000, and offshore platforms work in rough seas every day. The harder problems are building a whole settlement that is safe and affordable in open water, and working out whose laws apply. The movement's attempts so far show both. An agreement with French Polynesia collapsed in 2018, and the first single-family seastead, off Phuket, was seized by the Thai navy in 2019. The most advanced design, Oceanix Busan, plans a floating community for 12,000 residents and visitors moored in a sheltered harbour, but it has stalled since 2024. Residential cruise ships already carry permanent residents, but no settlement built for the purpose has housed permanent residents in open water.
