A small modular reactor is a nuclear power plant shrunk to a fraction of the usual size (300 megawatts or less) and built largely from standard factory-made parts rather than as a one-off giant construction project. The hope is that building many identical units will make nuclear power cheaper and quicker to build, and small enough to sit beside factories, data centres or towns that a big plant would overwhelm.

The idea is old, since navies have run small reactors for decades, but civilian SMRs are only now arriving. Russia runs a floating SMR plant in the Arctic, and China's gas-cooled HTR-PM has been in commercial operation since 2023. The first land-based, water-cooled SMR built for the grid, China's 125-megawatt Linglong One, is built and in hot testing ahead of loading fuel, running behind its planned 58-month build. Canada has begun building a GE-Hitachi BWRX-300, and in the US several advanced test reactors went critical in 2026.
