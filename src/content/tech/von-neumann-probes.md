A von Neumann probe is a spacecraft that, on arriving at an asteroid, moon or planet, mines local material, builds copies of itself and sends them onward. Because each probe makes more, a handful could in principle explore a whole galaxy, and the same machinery could build industry in space without hauling everything up from Earth.

The idea goes back to John von Neumann's theory of machines that contain a description of themselves and can build a copy. In 1980 Robert Freitas sketched a probe that would land a seed factory on a moon of a distant gas giant and, after about a thousand years of building, launch a new probe. Real progress is in fragments: 3D printers designed to print their own parts, and NASA's MOXIE, which made oxygen from Martian air in 2021. No machine has yet copied itself from raw materials, even on Earth, and that is the next real step. The first probes would most likely replicate within the Solar System, long before any reached another star.
