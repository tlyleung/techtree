Green steel replaces the coal and coke used to strip oxygen from iron ore with hydrogen made by splitting water with clean electricity. The iron is then melted into steel in electric arc furnaces, so the process emits mostly water instead of carbon dioxide. Steelmaking is one of the largest industrial sources of carbon dioxide, so cleaning it up would be among the biggest single steps in decarbonising industry.

The chemistry is proven. Sweden's HYBRIT pilot made hydrogen-reduced iron in 2021, delivered the first steel from it to Volvo that year, and had produced thousands of tonnes by 2024. The hard part is scale and cost: a commercial plant needs electrolysers of hundreds of megawatts and cheap, steady clean power. Stegra's Boden mill, designed for 2.5 million tonnes a year, nearly ran out of money before a 2026 rescue and has no fixed start date. Other European hydrogen-steel projects, such as ArcelorMittal's in Germany, have been shelved. Electrolysis of iron ore itself, pursued by Boston Metal, is a newer alternative still at demonstration-cell scale. If Boden starts and ramps up as planned, the first million tonnes could arrive before 2030.
