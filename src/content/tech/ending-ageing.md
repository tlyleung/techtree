Ending ageing means treating ageing itself rather than its diseases one at a time: stopping or reversing the slow damage that makes each year of adult life riskier than the last. Today a person's risk of dying rises steeply with every year of adult life; a therapy that held it flat would let people stay as healthy at ninety as at thirty, and could delay many of the diseases of old age, such as cancer, heart disease and dementia, at once. Researchers can already slow ageing in the lab. Single gene changes double the lifespan of worms, the drug rapamycin lengthens the lives of mice, and briefly reactivating embryonic genes has made old mouse tissue behave younger. In 2026 Life Biosciences began a human trial of such a reprogramming therapy, for an eye disease. Yet no treatment has stopped ageing in any mammal, and proving it in people would take years of careful follow-up. This is a long-range goal, and one many scientists doubt can be reached.
