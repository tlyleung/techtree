A Moon base would turn lunar exploration from brief visits into a place people live. Its value is partly practical, since local resources such as polar ice could one day supply water, oxygen and propellant, and partly strategic, as a proving ground for living on Mars and for science that needs a long stay.

The test here is demanding: someone living on the surface every day for at least a year. The transport leg is coming back. Artemis II took four astronauts around the Moon in April 2026, NASA plans crewed landings near the south pole from 2028, and China is testing the rocket and capsule for a landing before 2030. The ISS shows that rotating crews can keep a station permanently occupied. But the hard parts of a lunar outpost, including habitats, night-time power such as a fission reactor, and mining ice, exist only as plans and early designs. NASA's 2026 plan and the China-Russia ILRS both describe bases built in phases, without firm dates for continuous occupation.
