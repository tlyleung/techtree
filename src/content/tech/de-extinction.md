De-extinction means bringing back the traits, and perhaps the ecological role, of an animal that has died out. The most watched attempt is Colossal Biosciences' plan to edit Asian elephant cells with genes from the woolly mammoth, clone an embryo and carry it to term in a surrogate, producing a cold-hardy elephant with a mammoth's coat and fat. Supporters hope such animals could help restore Arctic grassland and lend tools to the conservation of endangered elephants; critics argue that an edited elephant isn't a mammoth.

The parts have been shown one at a time. A cloned bucardo, an extinct wild goat, was born alive, though it lived only minutes. Colossal has made elephant stem cells, mice with mammoth-like hair and gray wolves edited to resemble dire wolves. Nobody has yet reported an edited elephant embryo or pregnancy, and the company's timeline has slipped from 2028 to the early 2030s.
