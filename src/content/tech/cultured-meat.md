Cultured meat (also called cultivated or lab-grown meat) is real animal meat grown from cells in steel tanks rather than raised on a farm. If it can be made cheaply, it could supply meat with far less land, fewer emissions and no slaughter, and it would make meat supply less vulnerable to animal disease.

The science works: the first lab-grown burger was eaten in London in 2013, Singapore approved cultured chicken in 2020, and the US followed in 2023. Since then products have reached a few restaurants and, in late 2025, a first US grocery shelf. What hasn't been solved is cost. Growing cells at the density and scale of a meat-packing plant needs cheap growth media, huge sterile bioreactors and customers willing to commit before prices fall. Industry analysts say parity is in reach, but several start-ups have shut down for lack of funding, and Florida banned sales outright in 2024. This page's test is the commercial one: cultured meat on ordinary shelves at the price of the real thing.
