Utility fog is J. Storrs Hall's 1993 idea for a material made of billions of tiny robots, each with arms that grip its neighbours. Acting together, the robots could hold any shape, feel solid or let you walk through them, and rearrange on command, turning one substance into furniture, tools or a protective cushion. Kurzweil and others have pictured it as the physical counterpart of virtual reality: matter you can program.

Robot swarms have taken real first steps. A thousand small robots have arranged themselves into set shapes, swarms of simple 'particles' have moved as one, and in 2025 a collective of centimetre-sized robots flowed like a liquid and then locked into a solid that held a heavy load. Utility fog needs all of that in units smaller than a millimetre, each with its own power, sensing and grip, built and coordinated by the billion. That probably depends on mature molecular manufacturing, so it remains a distant but well-defined goal.
