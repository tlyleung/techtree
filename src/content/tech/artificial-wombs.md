An artificial womb would let a fetus keep developing outside the body, floating in warm synthetic amniotic fluid and fed oxygen and nutrients through its umbilical cord, as it would in the uterus. The first goal is medical: babies born at 22 to 24 weeks have lungs too immature to breathe air, and many die or suffer lasting harm in conventional incubators. Further off, carrying a pregnancy largely outside the body could help people who can't carry a child, and would raise deep ethical and legal questions.

The technology works in animals. Lambs have grown for four weeks in fluid-filled bags, and mouse embryos have developed for days in rotating bottles. US regulators discussed how to run the first human trials in 2023, and developers such as Vitara Biomedical are preparing, but as of late 2026 no human trial has been reported. Supporting a baby for the whole last third of pregnancy remains well beyond anything yet shown.
