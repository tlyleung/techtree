A xenotransplant puts an animal organ into a person. Pigs are the donors of choice because their organs are about the right size, and modern gene editing can knock out the pig genes that trigger violent rejection, add human genes that calm the immune system and remove pig viruses. If it works reliably, it could end the shortage of donor kidneys and hearts that leaves thousands of people on waiting lists, and many on dialysis for years.

Progress in living patients has been rapid. Two pig hearts, in 2022 and 2023, kept their recipients alive for weeks. Since 2024 several patients have received gene-edited pig kidneys, and in November 2026 one recipient passed a full year without dialysis. Formal trials cleared by the US FDA began in 2025, aiming at a licence application. This page now tracks the step that would make pig organs a real treatment: regulatory approval, or a pivotal trial that meets its goal.
