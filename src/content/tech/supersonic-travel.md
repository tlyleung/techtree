Supersonic airliners flew for 27 years: Concorde carried passengers across the Atlantic at twice the speed of sound from 1976 until its retirement in 2003. A new generation aims to bring that speed back and make it pay. Boom Supersonic is designing Overture, a 60-to-80-seat airliner for Mach 1.7 with its own Symphony engine, and holds orders and options from United, American and Japan Airlines. Its XB-1 demonstrator went supersonic in 2025, the first independently built civil jet to do so, and NASA's X-59 is testing whether a quieter sonic thump could make supersonic flight over land acceptable; a 2025 US executive order told the FAA to lift the overland ban, which would open many more routes. The airliner itself is still on paper and in parts: the first Symphony engine core, shared with a power-station turbine, was only assembled in late 2026, and the launch customer expects service later than 2029. Halving long-haul flight times remains within reach, but probably not this decade.
