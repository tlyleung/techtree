Photosynthesis in most crops wastes much of the sunlight that falls on them: leaves are slow to adjust when clouds pass, and the enzyme Rubisco often grabs oxygen instead of carbon dioxide, forcing the plant into a costly clean-up process called photorespiration. Fixing these inefficiencies could raise yields without more land, water or fertiliser, at a time when demand for food keeps rising.

Field trials have given promising but inconsistent results. Engineered tobacco grew markedly more in the field, and a 2022 soybean trial reported up to a third more seed, but other scientists disputed that trial and a 2026 trial of one of the same lines found no yield gain. Rice overexpressing a regulator gene yielded much more, though that works by changing many traits at once. A related goal is cereals that supply their own nitrogen, as a Mexican maize landrace does with help from root bacteria.

The next step is gains that hold up across many places and seasons in independent hands, followed by a submission to regulators.
