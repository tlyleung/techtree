Asteroid mining means extracting useful materials from asteroids: water that can be split into rocket fuel and air, and metals such as iron, nickel and the platinum group. Water mined in space could refuel spacecraft without lifting it out of Earth's gravity, and supporters argue that metals from space could one day ease the environmental cost of mining on Earth.

So far, only space agencies have touched asteroids, and only for science. Japan's Hayabusa2 blasted a crater in Ryugu and brought samples home, and NASA's OSIRIS-REx returned a sample of Bennu, but the totals are measured in grams. AstroForge, the most active company, lost its first probe in 2025; its next, DeepSpace-2, is built to rendezvous with a near-Earth asteroid and image it, with launch now expected around 2027. A first private landing, followed by extraction of a kilogram or more, are the next real tests; selling the material profitably is a further step.
