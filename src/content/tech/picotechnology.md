Picotechnology is the speculative step after nanotechnology: engineering not with whole atoms but with structure inside or beneath them, at the scale of trillionths of a metre. Ray Kurzweil predicted that 'picoengineering' would become practical in 2072. If it proved possible, it could yield materials and machines with properties ordinary matter cannot have, far denser computing and a new frontier for physics-based engineering.

Today it is a hope with no design behind it. Atom-scale engineering is real: scientists have placed single atoms since 1990 and built a transistor from one atom in 2012. Instruments can now see atoms with under twenty picometres of blur, and lasers have begun to switch the states of a thorium nucleus, the basis of a future nuclear clock. But these use the natural properties of atoms and nuclei. Nobody yet knows how to build a stable, designed structure below the atomic scale, so this page sets a strict test and treats the date as highly uncertain.
