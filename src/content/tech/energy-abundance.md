---

Energy abundance means a world whose electricity comes almost entirely from sources that don't emit carbon: sun, wind, water, the atom and the heat of the Earth. Passing 90% would make cheap, clean power the default everywhere, and open the way to electrify transport, heating and industry without adding to the climate problem.

All the generating technologies already exist, and some countries are there: France generated 95% of its electricity from nuclear and renewable sources in 2024. The world as a whole is a long way behind. The IEA puts low-carbon sources at about 42% of global electricity today, and in its policy-based scenarios renewables reach only 45-50% by 2035.

The hard part is the last stretch on grids without big rivers or reactors, where wind and solar need storage and firm clean power to cover calm, dark weeks. New options are arriving: enhanced geothermal went commercial in Utah in 2026, and perovskite tandem solar panels began shipping in 2024. Whether they scale fast enough is the open question.
